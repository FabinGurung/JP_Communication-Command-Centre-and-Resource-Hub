01 · Learn the evidence hierarchy

What do we actually know?

Do not let an attractive site photo turn assumptions into engineering or legal facts.

Do not pay a non-refundable advance yet. A verified plot boundary, clear title, legal road access, dry-season water, municipal land-use permission, NEA clearance and a hillside engineering review are all still needed.

Four phrases that are easy to confuse

Road centreline (सडकको केन्द्रबिन्दु) The surveyed middle of the relevant road alignment. A distance measured from here is not the same as a setback measured from the edge of the paved road.
Road right-of-way (ROW / सडक अधिकार क्षेत्र) The road/public-use corridor, which may extend beyond the visible track. Obtain its approved mapping and classification.
Building setback (सेटब्याक) The required space between the legally established boundary/ROW and the building. Whether it adds to a centreline allowance is a question for the rural municipality—not a number to guess.
Power-line ROW versus conductor clearance ROW is an entire restricted corridor, often metres wide on both sides of a transmission centreline; conductor clearance is distance from the actual moving/sagging wire to a house or tree.

Rule 52 — power wires over houses The regulations generally prohibit electric lines directly above houses, with a limited low-voltage exception for certain garages/sheds/walls and specified vertical clearance. Ask NEA about the exact structure and route before planning a roof.
Rule 66 — restricted land use Land under and near regulated power lines can remain in private ownership while buildings and trees are restricted. Ownership and buildability are different questions.
05 · Wells, public sources, septic and slopes

Water and well maapdanda is not one number

A private dug well, public traditional tap, septic tank, soak pit, river and irrigation canal are different cases. Avoid mixing boundary clearances with drinking-water contamination safeguards.

ScenarioGuidanceHow it is measuredEvidence tier
Useful technical rule-of-thumb: ENPHO's Nepal sanitation manual recommends about 20 m horizontal and 4 m vertical separation between wastewater disposal and a water source, and 30 m horizontal and 4 m vertical from soak pits to wells or springs. These are engineering recommendations, not verified municipal statutory distances. An uphill source, groundwater flow, rock fractures and rain may require a different design. Read the manual ↗

For Astam's reported dry-season water problem

1. Inspect in the driest season

Ask two neighbors for actual daily supply, tanker frequency, outages and spring reduction. The wet-season photo tells little about February–April reliability.

2. Establish the right to the water

Request a written users' committee connection, source-owner agreement or lawful borewell permission—before assuming a new well can replace a failed spring.

3. Price the backup

Determine pump head, power outages, storage size, filters, replacement/maintenance and whether a tanker can reach the plot in the monsoon.

07 · Deal strategy

A safe decision sequence

Gate 1 — Before token

Malpot title and Rokka check, seller authority, official Kitta map, local land-use/road determination, NEA line status and dry-season water evidence.

Gate 2 — Before contract

Topographical demarcation, geotechnical observations, stormwater + septic design, legal access and realistic development cost. Ask a lawyer to draft refund conditions.

Gate 3 — Before transfer

Registration documents, tax, seller's consent, bank-traceable payments, signed certificates, final survey reconciliation and updated holds/encumbrances check.

No purchase approval has been issued. Do not assume a government-planned road, public service pipeline, scenic view, or telephone assertion creates a property right. A signed statement from the appropriate authority carries more weight than a broker's claims.
