Before you buy: understand the actual rules.

A field guide written for a land buyer. Compare local road मापदण्ड, building setbacks, high-tension line clearances, wells and septic separation and an expanded 46-question buyer checklist. Every number has an evidence tier: local publication, national law, model bylaw, or engineering advice.

Six essential terms, without jargon

Road centreline — सडकको केन्द्रबिन्दु

A surveyed centre of the legally relevant road. Five metres from centre is NOT five metres from the asphalt edge.

Right of way — सडक अधिकार क्षेत्र

The corridor reserved for public access, often wider than a visible track. Its legal boundary must be surveyed.

Building setback — सेटब्याक

Distance from the legal plot or road-ROW boundary to the building. It may be separate from centreline restrictions.

High-tension corridor versus conductor clearance

Wire-to-wall clearance is not the same as the full width of restricted land beside a transmission line.

Lalpurja · Shresta · Kitta · Napi

Title, ownership records, parcel identity, mapped dimensions, and physical boundary demarcation must reconcile.

Well offset versus drinking-water safety

A well one metre from a boundary does NOT imply a nearby septic or soak pit is safe for drinking water.

01Road centreline, municipal ROW and accessOfficial Machhapuchhre Ward 6 road list, published 2018

In the municipality's published road list, उदाउने ढुंगा–अस्ताम बाटो appears as 5 m from the road centreline. Other named roads in Ward 6 have 4 m or 6 m figures. A 2018 publication is not proof the rule remains unchanged, or that your plot adjoins the listed Astam route.

Ask in writing: Which named route passes this Kitta? Where is its surveyed centreline and legally reserved ROW? Does another building setback apply beyond the listed distance, or is it already included? Check any new road-widening/alignment plan.

02Building setbacks by type of useNational reference manual — NOT confirmed Ward 6 enactment

A private villa, mixed commercial/residential building, taller building and public structure can have different setbacks. The government's reference book contains examples, but the municipal engineering office must confirm today's controlling rules and four sides of the actual plot.

Ask for front, rear, both side setbacks, height, ground coverage, FAR, land classification, permitted use, road line and naksa-pass eligibility. Some tall-building reference values conflict internally; do not select the lesser one without a certified municipal reply.
03Electric power lines — three separate safety distancesConductor-to-building, conductor-to-ground, and transmission ROW

A. Horizontal separation from live conductor to house/tree

B. Minimum conductor height above roads and ground

C. Full restricted transmission right-of-way

Do not assume 2 metres from a pole is sufficient. Rule 50 specifies different conductor clearances, while transmission corridors can be much wider. Older project evidence and newer reporting conflict for 66 kV (12 m versus 18 m total). Ask NEA for the actual line voltage, conductor route, swing, sag, transmission ROW and written building clearance. Rules 48, 50, 52, 66 are relevant.
04Well / public tap / septic / soak pit / river setbacksGovernment model guidance versus ENPHO technical advice

A private dug well, a public water tap, a septic tank and a soak pit are different cases. They cannot all be assigned the same arbitrary distance. If water is unreliable during the dry season, test source rights and supply before buying.

Critical water-quality distinction: a government reference illustrates a 1 m private-well boundary offset and 4 m building offset near a public well/tap. ENPHO separately advises 20 m horizontal + 4 m vertical between wastewater disposal and a water source, and 30 m + 4 m for a soak pit. The latter are engineering recommendations; a slope, fractured rock or spring may need a stricter case-specific design.
